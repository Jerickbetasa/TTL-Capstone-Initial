Abubakar, Midzmar

II - 12 BSE Math

My overall experience for the activities done under TTL including the group's TEIM, the website where collection of ideas were made, the presentation and evaluation that greatly focuses on the interaction of education and the technology, is deeply meaningful that enhances my understanding regarding the way I view technology in education. One of the realizations that comes to my mind is that we always opt for beautiful designs that capture student's interest but behind those ideas, one consideration must be looked out, it is whether it targets the learning goals, and the way the student understands the lesson, even if the lesson seems complex and needs deeper analysis in which our group successfully worked on it. I have learnt the way how technology works with the aid of the group’s knowledge. I also learned that the overall designs and the way the lesson proper is presented should be balanced so it ensures the students will benefit from it. The way I communicate with other people and the way I present my ideas are strengthened and deeply assessed. From planning to gathering data, each of us has distinctive roles, where every part has the meaningful experience that lies behind. Throughout the course, I realized that technology must be intentionally designed, that means we must plan it proactively and know whether it will motivate students more to learn or get distracted by the technology itself. Also, we must come to pinpoint that technology must support active learning, deepen understanding from complex ones to understandable ones and make topics basic and feasible wherein one of the topics our group chose is the Special Products and Factoring, breaking it down to step by step without getting distracted by the design plays a major role for students learning. I also learned that throughout the course, major questions such as “Does this material really help students learn, or is it just pleasant to the eyes? Are our instructions clear enough so students can easily follow it” must really be considered. Overall, I believe that being an instructional designer requires continuous reflection and feedback coming from others to ensure that our lesson is understandable by the bigger audience. Technology should never be added as another hindrance, instead it must serve a clear purpose such as removing learning barriers, targeting lesson objectives, and ensuring that every learner, regardless of their background, has an equal chance to succeed. That is what I learnt the most in the course of TTL.

Basco, Alejandro

II - 12 BSE Math

We have always held the notion that knowledge is power. But when does knowledge truly become power? Being knowledgeable in the fields we engage in comes with the responsibility of knowing when and how to apply that knowledge. Power comes when we begin to recognize its strengths and weaknesses. This is where the argument arises: not all knowledge we possess comes with power, but every power we possess begins with knowledge. Technology, for example, is a powerful tool on its own, but its essence becomes meaningless when we do not know how to use it properly. In the modern day, society has progressed into a world of technology where digitalization has become inevitable. This is evident in various fields, including agriculture, industry, economics, and even education. With a passion for teaching, I have to continuously adapt to the transformation of our nation because I am part of its foundation. The role of teachers in our society has become increasingly connected to technology, and as we dive deeper into its core, there are many things we have to consider, factors we need to be mindful of, and skills we must master in order to become effective users of technology. This information, as a future teacher, provides me with a guide to navigate the modern world through the lens of technology. We have reached a point where the digital world has become the community we live in, and as teachers, we have to integrate technology into education while keeping everything humanized. This comes with great responsibility, as one wrong decision in using technology can create a domino effect in our society. This course has taught me how to become a flexible pre-service teacher who is not afraid to adapt or even recreate approaches in education. I may not have focused solely on the content of the course, but this journey has made me realize that teachers have the option to remain traditional or embrace modernization. However, for me, a wiser approach is to combine both in ways that are effectively utilized while taking the necessary conditions and factors in consideration. We must always remember to be responsible users and wise producers as we navigate the world of technology, especially in the field of education.

Betasa, Jerick

II - 12 BSE Math

We talked about education as the key to unlocking our fullest potential, reaching our dreams, and make an impact in our community. But access to it has been a problem for many years. Lack of equipment, crowded rooms, lack of teachers, lack of classrooms, and even lack of support for students to maintain their studies still happen. I have witnessed some students choose to drop out and some continue to endure the hardships. And as our world progresses into digital transformation, lots of our students, teachers, and schools are being left out. Lack of access to technology and illiteracy continues to widen the gap in access to quality education. Before this program, I thought of technology as something that is only for elites, technology that makes learning high-tech, and fun. Typical lessons we experience are often just a plain presentations and demonstration. Technology is cool for me. But, Learning about Technology and Teaching Literacy is not just about learning how to integrate technology inside classrooms. It is about learning how we can utilize technology to make education much more accessible for all students. Whether you live in the city or you live in rural areas where the us of technology to empower learning is still uncommon, technology can help make education more accessible. I am very grateful to be here in this program. Learning things I didn’t know before like learning about responsible technology use, digital literacy, and the different ways technology can support learning made me question about how can I play my part, as a teacher, to use technology to empower my students regardless of their background. Having vast knowledge about using technology won’t help if you are just using it to make your lesson or class seemingly high-tech. The real, positive impact lies in how do we use that knowledge to make the class meaningful, inclusive, and impactful to our students. I realized that even if you are good at programming or integrating technology in your discussion to make you look cool, it is much more cooler when utilizing technology helps our student, especially the underprivileged one, to participate inside the classroom. I also realized the importance of responsible use of technology. Technology is a tool. It can maybe enhance your skill set as a teacher, or it could reveal your skill issue. Utilizing technology with no clear goals and meaningful follow ups can make the class bland and meaningless. As a teacher, technology should empower our learning strategies, not overshadow us. And when using technology, it is very important to always make responsible choices that protect privacy, promote fairness, and prevent harm to our students. Technology is great to assessing our students, enhancing discussion, and reshaping the learning experience. But how can we create a fair, safe, and meaningful learning experience if we compromise the privacy and safety of our learners? One thing I realized is that we create a much more effective and meaningful learning experience if we respect the privacy of our students. What is the value of technology to empower our students if we expose them to digital harms we are supposed to protect them from? The use of technology for learning should feel safe and promote fairness. As the world transforms into a much digitalized world, integrating technology not only makes the lesson more flashy, high-tech, or futuristic, it makes the learning more purposeful and significant to our lesson. But, integrating technology also carries a risk. And as a teacher, our decision and actions we take create an impact that would define the future of our students. And as access of education continues to be limited for many underprivileged students, we, the teachers, should not just adapt to the transformation brought by digitalization, but break down the barriers that keep our classrooms divided.

Dela Cruz, Ylaiza

II - 12 BSE Math

Technology is now vastly used worldwide, where students nowadays mostly depend on the resources it could give in support of their learning. As teachers, we must learn how to adjust ourselves, or our teaching methods rather, in this fast-paced and technology-inclined world. Additionally, as future teachers of the students in the 21st century, the course Technology for Teaching and Learning is very important that can serve as your pre-existing knowledge once you are in the field of teaching actual students. In my personal experience as a student of this course, it helped me a lot in knowing how to use technology properly in teaching without putting the risk of my students’ learning. It made me see the point of view of my students, where I questioned myself what would happen if I showed this specific type of technology to my students? Will it actually help or support their learning? If there is one thing that left a mark on me in this course, it will be the one I discussed myself during our group’s session facilitation. It is “Pedagogy first, Technology second.” This phrase is important especially for us, the next teachers of future generations. We must carefully first consider how our students are going to learn from our lesson, regardless of the involvement of technology. As teachers we must avoid using “Tech for tech’s sake” where we prioritize its use rather than the goal or achieving the objectives of the lesson. In conclusion, Technology for Teaching and Learning is a course that could change a whole different batch of students on how they learned from their teachers. As future teachers, we must never forget what were the things that it taught us in our own perceived ways. As time goes by, different types of technology are still being developed, and the top prioritized concern of a teacher must be: Will this technology actually help my students’ learning?

Delos Santos, Jeremie

II - 12 BSE Math

Teaching and learning are continuously evolving alongside modern technology. While technology offers unprecedented convenience in the classroom, it operates like a two-sided coin—bringing significant advantages alongside distinct drawbacks. Integrating digital tools into teaching should never be driven solely by convenience; it must be a purposeful and beneficial implementation for all learners. The course Technology for Teaching and Learning guides pre-service teachers to effectively integrate digital tools that meet learning objectives without compromising instructional quality. While convenience is a factor, technology selection must prioritize digital equity and accessibility. Rather than focusing on aesthetic appeal, educators must evaluate tools based on their functionality and alignment with student needs. Furthermore, financial constraints and varying levels of digital literacy among students mean that access is not equal. Educators must remember that technology exists to support learning, and although options are abundant, only specific tools are truly suitable for targeted educational outcomes. Additionally, teachers must act as guardians in the digital space. In today’s fast-paced virtual environment, students are increasingly vulnerable to online risks. This reality calls on educators to cultivate critical thinking, analytical skills, and ethical behavior in students, empowering them to navigate technology safely and respectfully. Finally, teachers must promote digital health and wellness. By understanding the physical and psychological impacts of screen time and device usage, educators can ensure that technology integration supports, rather than harms, students' overall well-being. Technology in education is not a substitute for effective pedagogy, nor is its value measured by novelty or visual appeal. True educational technology serves as an equitable bridge to learning—one that meets diverse needs, protects student well-being, and empowers learners to navigate the digital world ethically and critically.
